Common Tattoo Questions

These are the questions we hear most often at Hyper Inkers, from choosing a shop to planning your visit.

To choose the best tattoo shop in San Antonio, work through the six checks below.

  • Review each artist’s portfolio in the exact style you want, healed photos as well as fresh ones, since a shop that is strong in traditional work is not always right for fine line or realism.
  • Read recent client reviews past the star rating, looking for specifics on clean linework, how the tattoo healed, and how the studio handled booking and aftercare.
  • Confirm hygiene in person, from single-use needles opened in front of you to an autoclave for reusable tools and fresh gloves at every station.
  • Check that each artist specializes in a style rather than accepting every request, because a specialist who tattoos your style daily works more consistently than a generalist.
  • Ask for a consultation before you commit, and judge how carefully the artist listens, plans placement, and explains the price.
  • Weigh price against the work, not against the cheapest quote, since a rushed cheap tattoo often costs more to fix or cover later.

Look for an artist whose portfolio shows your style done well and done often, not just one or two examples. Healed photos matter more than fresh ones, because a tattoo that still looks clean months later proves the linework and shading were done right. Check that the artist tattoos full-time and designs custom work rather than only applying flash, and read whether clients mention clear communication and a calm, unrushed process. The right artist for a portrait is rarely the one you would pick for fine line or lettering, so match the artist to the exact piece you have in mind.

Walk away from a studio that looks unclean, reuses needles, or cannot show you sealed, single-use equipment. Other warning signs are an artist with no portfolio in your style, a quote that seems far too cheap for custom work, and pressure to book on the spot without a consultation. Be cautious if no one will answer questions about sterilization, if the artist wants to copy someone else’s tattoo exactly, or if reviews repeatedly mention infections or crooked lines. A shop that hesitates to show its process is telling you something.

How long a tattoo takes depends on its size, detail, and placement. A small, simple design can be finished in under an hour, a medium piece often runs two to four hours, and large or highly detailed work, like a full sleeve or a back piece, is split across several sessions. Fine detail and heavy shading add time. Your artist gives you a realistic time estimate at your consultation, so you know how long to set aside.

A cover-up draws new artwork over old ink, while removal fades the tattoo over multiple sessions. Choose a cover-up for a fresh design, or removal when you want to lighten the tattoo first.

Come rested and hydrated, and eat a full meal within a couple of hours of your appointment so your blood sugar stays steady. Avoid alcohol for 24 hours beforehand and go easy on caffeine, since both can thin your blood and increase bleeding. Wear comfortable clothing that gives the artist easy access to the area, and bring a photo ID plus any reference images. Moisturize the skin in the days before, but not on the day itself.
